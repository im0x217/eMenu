# Copy, Typography, Category Bias, and Quality Loop

Readability rules, domain biases, regeneration triggers, the 27-point quality audit, and response execution sequence.

## Copy, Readability, and Typography Rules

### Text & Copy Discipline
Write short, believable, product-real copy: concise headlines, crisp CTA labels, and tight supporting lines (especially on screen 1 and onboarding). Eliminate lorem ipsum, paragraph walls, inspirational filler, and overly technical labels.

### Text Size & Contrast (Non-Negotiable)
**If text feels small, the design is not finished yet.**
- Prioritize comfortably readable titles, body copy, labels, and button text at normal viewing size.
- Maintain clear contrast and generous padding around text blocks; protect text over imagery with clean fades/masks.
- If typography becomes cramped, simplify layout, trim content, increase spacing, enlarge type, split into another screen, or regenerate. **Readable beats clever, dense, or decorative small type.**

### Typography Hierarchy
Build clear contrast between headline, section header, body, and supporting label using disciplined weights, readable mobile scale, clean line wrapping, and restrained font moods (never one uniform weight or oversized drama on every screen).

### Spacing & Density Rhythm
Keep internal padding generous and blocks cleanly separated so the UI feels composed and touch-friendly. Balance denser functional screens with calmer breather screens across a flow.

## Category-Specific Biases

- **Fintech:** Trust, calm spacing, clear tabular figures, restrained accents, minimal fake charts, crisp transaction clarity, subtle texture.
- **Health / Fitness:** Calm structure, strong metric hierarchy, motivating non-noisy progress modules, airy spacing, optimistic wellness texture/imagery.
- **Productivity:** Clarity, list/card discipline, simple navigation, calm density, strong task hierarchy, minimal premium supporting visuals.
- **Social:** Profile and feed rhythm, purposeful media moments, clear creation vs. browsing hierarchy, stronger flow variety and image direction.
- **Commerce:** Browse/detail/cart clarity, strong product photography, stable product card aspect ratios, clean checkout hierarchy, editorial treatments.
- **Wellness / Lifestyle:** Soft tactile materials, calm typography, low visual noise, generous breathing room, elegant imagery and soft fades.

## Regeneration Rule

Never accept the first mediocre render. Regenerate immediately if:
1. Text is too small or unreadable.
2. Spacing is cramped, uneven, or unclear.
3. Navigation feels fake or overloaded.
4. The screen looks like a shrunken website in a phone.
5. Onboarding screens look like cloned template slides.
6. Image framing/aspect ratios drift across screens.
7. Cards stack into box-in-box clutter.
8. The first screen is noisy or lacks a clear focal point.
9. The flow lacks screen-to-screen variation or breaks design-bible consistency.
10. Backgrounds look sterile/flat or textures/fades muddy the text.
11. Imagery feels lazy, missing, or disconnected.
12. Decorative assets are absent/bland or noisy/overdone.
13. Color palette feels generic, muddy, or cliché.
14. Design feels boringly oversimplified or lacks identity.
15. Device mockup framing is uneven, cropped, sloppy, or overpowers the UI.

## Quality Check

Verify all 27 items internally before finalizing output:
1. Does this feel like a real mobile app, not a website in a phone?
2. Are safe areas respected visually?
3. Is the first screen clean enough?
4. Is the copy short enough?
5. Is the type readable?
6. Are there enough screens for the requested flow?
7. Were too few screens generated out of laziness?
8. If a detail was unclear, was a fresh detail render created (not cropped)?
9. Is the app free of obvious mobile AI tells?
10. Is the layout free of box-in-box clutter?
11. Are image moments purposeful and consistent?
12. Does the flow feel coherent?
13. Do screens vary enough without breaking the design system?
14. Does the product feel premium and app-native?
15. Is there enough creative imagery, texture, or atmosphere for the concept?
16. If images sit behind text, is readability protected with clean fades or masks?
17. Are decorative assets clean and restrained?
18. Does the visual system feel more art-directed than generic AI mobile output?
19. Is the color palette clean and controlled?
20. Does the design feel non-generic?
21. Is the design clean without being boringly oversimplified?
22. Do all screens clearly belong to the same app?
23. Is the flow logical from screen to screen?
24. Is the phone mockup framing clean and evenly padded on all sides?
25. Is the text comfortably readable and not too small?
26. Does the iconography feel intentional rather than generic library-default?
27. Is the phone border/mockup present and clean without stealing attention from screen content?

## Response Behavior Sequence

1. Infer app category, platform mode, and required screen count.
2. Select visual direction, image art direction bias, texture treatment, decorative assets, and palette logic.
3. Lock internal App Design Bible for multi-screen consistency.
4. Generate all required screen images (plus extra screens/detail renders whenever clarity benefits).
5. Keep screen 1 extra clean; avoid website layouts and nested-card clutter.
6. Apply purposeful imagery, subtle textures, fades/masks, generous spacing, legible type, controlled palettes, and custom-feeling icons.
7. Present inside clean, subtle, evenly margined phone mockups (content-first).
8. Audit all 27 quality checks, refine/regenerate any weak screen, and output the final screen set without switching into code mode.

## Example Interpretations

- **"make a premium fitness app":** Pick iOS-native or cross-platform neutral; generate multi-screen flow with clean first screen, calm metric hierarchy, zero fake chart spam, subtle wellness texture/imagery, readable numbers, and consistent phone mockups.
- **"design a 5-screen ecommerce app":** Generate 5 distinct standalone screens (browse, detail, cart, checkout, confirmation) with strong product photography in fixed media frames, editorial fades where helpful, consistent device framing, and custom-feeling icons.
- **"make an onboarding flow for a social app":** Generate multiple varied onboarding screens (never identical slides) with tight copy, an uncluttered first screen, creative image-behind-text fade treatments, distinctive controlled palette, readable type, and balanced mockups.

## Final Goal

Deliver mobile app screen concepts and flows that are app-native, readable, structured, non-generic, tactically textured, visually coherent across every screen, and framed cleanly in content-first phone mockups — strictly as images, never code.
